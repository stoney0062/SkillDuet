THE IDEA

Better models.
Better skills.
Learning together.

Experience can become part of a model’s parameters, or live outside the model as reusable textual skills. But as the model learns, the skills it needs also change.

SkillDuet connects these two forms of learning. Local skills extracted from recent trajectories guide on-policy distillation. Model response feedback then selects which skills to consolidate into persistent Global guidance.

The updated model and its evolving skills shape the next round of experience—closing the loop between internalization and consolidation.

METHOD

Learn continuously.
Consolidate selectively.

The model supplies two kinds of response feedback: token-level supervision for learning, and a trajectory-level signal for deciding what to retain.

01

Extract Local skills

An external analyzer compares an eligible successful trajectory with a failed one, turning recent experience into shared procedural guidance.

02

Internalize with OPD

The policy scores the same sampled response with and without Local guidance. Confidence-gated token supervision complements the RL objective.

03

Consolidate into Global

Rank eligible candidates by cached Strength, keep the top half, and synthesize them with existing Global guidance every ten steps.

THE SELECTION SIGNAL

Keep what changes the response.

Strength measures the magnitude of a skill’s effect on held-out response log probabilities. Scores are cached when each skill is generated; scoring requires no extra environment interaction.

SELECTION CRITERION

The magnitude of the response shift matters.

In the historical ALFWorld candidate pool, Strength achieves 97.9% peak validation success and 88.26% normalized AUC, outperforming reward-weighted Utility, success–failure Contrast, and Random selection.

This earlier candidate-pool study is distinct from the main framework’s independent ten-step windows.
